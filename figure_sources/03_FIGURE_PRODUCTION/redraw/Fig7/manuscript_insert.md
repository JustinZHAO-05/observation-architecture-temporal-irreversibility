To characterize the reference dependence of the cycle statistic, we decomposed each recording's cross-half score into the product of an amplitude factor and a directional alignment. Let \(\mathbf v_A\) and \(\mathbf v_B\) contain the imaginary parts of the cycle products over the same frequency bins and channel triplets in the two halves. Then \(Q=\operatorname{RMS}(\mathbf v_A)\operatorname{RMS}(\mathbf v_B)\cos\varphi\), where \(\varphi\) is the angle between the two vectors. Across the 113 matched recordings, common-average referencing shifted the distributions of both factors (Fig. 7d). For eyes-open recordings, the mean amplitude factor rose from \(1.712\times10^{-3}\) to \(2.028\times10^{-3}\) and mean alignment from \(0.045\) to \(0.177\). For eyes-closed recordings, the corresponding means rose from \(1.901\times10^{-3}\) to \(2.295\times10^{-3}\) and from \(0.101\) to \(0.216\). These descriptive changes locate the reference dependence in the magnitude and cross-half alignment of the observed cycle arrays. In both conditions the group score is the mean of the recording-level products.
